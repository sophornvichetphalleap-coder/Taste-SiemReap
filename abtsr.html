A City Reborn

Siem Reap's journey from a quiet provincial town to a world-class destination.

Siem Reap literally means "Siam Defeated" — a name given in the 18th century after a Khmer victory over the Siamese army. For most of its history, it was a modest town on the Siem Reap River, quietly sitting beside the ruins of what was once the largest pre-industrial city in the world.

That all changed in the early 20th century, when French explorers "rediscovered" the temples of Angkor Wat and the surrounding archaeological park. Restoration work, then tourism, gradually transformed the town — but the real boom came after the 1990s, following decades of conflict.

Today, Siem Reap is a thriving city of around 250,000 people that welcomes over 2 million visitors a year. It balances ancient heritage with modern comforts: boutique hotels, world-class restaurants, night markets, and a food scene that ranges from $1 street noodles to award-winning fine dining.

But what makes Siem Reap truly special isn't just the temples. It's the people — their resilience, their warmth, and their deep pride in sharing Khmer culture with the world.

What Makes Siem Reap Special

The elements that define a visit to this remarkable city.

🛕

Angkor Wat & Temples

The largest religious monument in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Over 1,000 temples spread across 400 square kilometers, from the iconic Angkor Wat to the jungle-swallowed Ta Prohm.

🌟 UNESCO Site
🍜

Khmer Food Scene

From street-side Kuy Teav at dawn to refined Amok in colonial-style restaurants. Siem Reap is the best place in Cambodia to explore authentic Khmer cuisine — including Khmer Rouge survivor recipes and modern fusion.

🍽️ Food Capital
🌊

Tonle Sap Lake

The largest freshwater lake in Southeast Asia. Its unique flood cycle reverses the flow of the Tonle Sap River each year — home to floating villages, bird sanctuaries, and one of the world's richest freshwater fisheries.

💧 Natural Wonder
🎨

Arts & Crafts

Siem Reap is a hub for traditional Khmer crafts — silk weaving, stone carving, lacquerware, and shadow puppetry. Visit workshops in the Artisans Angkor complex or the night market for handmade souvenirs.

🎭 Living Heritage
🌿

Nature & Countryside

Beyond the temples lies rice paddies, lotus fields, and stilted villages. Cycling tours, cooking classes in rural homes, and sunset boat rides offer a quieter side of Siem Reap that most visitors miss.

🚲 Rural Escape
🌙

Night Markets & Pub Street

After sunset, the city comes alive. Pub Street offers bars and live music, while the Angkor Night Market and Made in Cambodia Market sell everything from silk scarves to spicy dried mango — all at fair, negotiable prices.

🎶 Evening Energy

Neighborhoods & Areas

Where to stay, eat, and explore around the city.

🏛️ Old Market Area

The historic heart of Siem Reap. Psychedelic temples, French-colonial shophouses, the covered market, and easy walking access to Pub Street and the river.

🌃 Pub Street & Nightlife

The backpacker and nightlife hub — bars, restaurants, live music, and late-night food. Great for atmosphere, though not the quietest place to sleep.

🌾 Riverside & Wat Bo

Quieter, leafy streets along the Siem Reap River. Home to boutique hotels, cafés, and many of the city's best Khmer restaurants.

🌳 Charles de Gaulle

The tree-lined boulevard leading to Angkor. Luxury resorts, fine dining, and the closest access to the temple park entrance.

🎨 Kandal Village

A small, trendy neighborhood with artisan boutiques, specialty coffee, and design-forward restaurants. Perfect for a slow afternoon.

🚤 Tonle Sap Edge

The floating villages and stilted communities around Kompong Khleang and Kompong Phluk — a 30–60 minute drive from the city center.

A Brief History

Key moments in the story of Siem Reap and the Angkor region.

802 AD

The Khmer Empire Begins

Jayavarman II unifies the Khmer kingdoms and establishes the Angkorian Empire, which would rule much of Southeast Asia for over 600 years.

1113 – 1150 AD

Angkor Wat Is Built

King Suryavarman II commissions Angkor Wat — the largest religious monument in the world — dedicated to the Hindu god Vishnu. It later becomes a Buddhist temple.

1431 AD

The Fall of Angkor

After repeated Siamese invasions, the Khmer capital moves south to Phnom Penh. Angkor is gradually abandoned to the jungle, though monks continue to tend its temples.

1860s

French Rediscovery

French explorer Henri Mouhot brings global attention to the Angkor ruins. Restoration work begins, and Siem Reap begins to grow as a base for visitors.

1975 – 1979

The Khmer Rouge Years

A devastating period for Cambodia. Cities are emptied, temples are damaged, and millions lose their lives. Siem Reap, like the rest of the country, suffers deeply.

1992

UNESCO World Heritage

Angkor is declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site. International restoration begins, and Siem Reap slowly reopens to tourism.

2000s – Today

A Modern Tourism Hub

Siem Reap becomes one of Southeast Asia's top destinations, attracting millions of visitors a year and building a thriving food, art, and hospitality scene.
