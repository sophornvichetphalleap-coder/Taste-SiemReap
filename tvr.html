Your Travel Guide to Siem Reap

From arrival tips to daily budgets, temple etiquette, transport, and the best times to eat — here's how to make the most of your trip to Siem Reap.

Before You Go

Practical essentials to sort out before you land in Siem Reap.

🛂

Visa & Entry

Most nationalities can get a visa on arrival (USD $30) or apply online via e-Visa. Bring a passport photo and ensure your passport is valid for 6+ months.

💵

Money & ATMs

Cambodia uses US dollars and Khmer Riel side by side. Small USD bills ($1–$5) are most useful. ATMs are widely available with ~$5 fees.

📶

SIM Cards

Pick up a local SIM at the airport or any convenience store. Tourist plans cost around $5 for 1 week of data — great for maps and translation.

🌤️

Best Time to Visit

November to March is cool and dry — the peak season. April to May is hot, and June to October is the green season with occasional rain.

💉

Health & Safety

Drink bottled water, use mosquito repellent at dawn and dusk, and carry basic medicines. Siem Reap is generally very safe for travelers.

📱

Useful Apps

Grab for transport, Google Maps for offline navigation, Google Translate for Khmer, and XE Currency for quick exchange rates.

Getting Around

How to move between temples, markets, and the city center — plus typical prices.

🛺

Tuk-Tuk

The classic Siem Reap ride. Expect $2–$4 within town, $15–$20 for a full day of temple-hopping. Always agree on the price before you ride.

🚗

Private Car / Grab

Best for longer temple circuits or hot days. Grab cars are metered and reliable, usually $4–$10 for city trips. Day rates start around $45.

🚲

Bicycle & E-Bike

Great for exploring the city and nearby temples. Rentals cost $1–$3 for bicycles and $8–$12 for e-bikes. Helmets are recommended.

🚶

Walking

The city center (Pub Street, Old Market, riverside) is very walkable. Most hotels and restaurants are within 15 minutes of each other.

Daily Budget

Approximate costs per person per day in Siem Reap (in USD).

🎒

Backpacker · $25–$40

Hostel dorm ($6–$10), street food meals ($1–$3), tuk-tuk to temples ($15 split), Angkor pass shared over days.

🏨

Mid-Range · $50–$90

Boutique guesthouse ($25–$45), restaurant meals ($3–$8), private tuk-tuk driver, one tour or cooking class.

✨

Comfort · $120+

4- or 5-star hotel ($70+), fine dining, private car with guide, spa treatments, and sunrise temple tours.

3-Day Itinerary

A balanced plan mixing temples, food, culture, and rest.

01

Arrival & Old Market Evening

Check into your hotel and take an easy first day. Explore the Old Market, walk along the river, and try your first Khmer dinner. End the night at Pub Street or a quiet rooftop bar.

🍜 Khmer dinner 🛍️ Old Market 🌙 Pub Street
02

Angkor Sunrise & Small Circuit

Start before dawn for sunrise at Angkor Wat. Then explore Bayon, Ta Prohm, and Banteay Kdei with a tuk-tuk driver. Break for lunch at a local stall and return to the hotel for an afternoon rest.

🌅 Angkor Wat 🗿 Bayon 🌿 Ta Prohm
03

Floating Village & Cooking Class

Take a morning trip to Kompong Khleang or Tonle Sap. In the afternoon, join a Khmer cooking class to learn dishes like Amok and fresh spring rolls. End with a farewell dinner at a trusted local restaurant.

🚤 Floating village 👩‍🍳 Cooking class 🍽️ Farewell dinner

Cultural Etiquette

A few small gestures go a long way in Cambodia.

🙏

Respect the Temples

Cover your shoulders and knees. Remove hats and shoes before entering shrines. Speak quietly and don't touch carvings or monks.

👋

The Sampeah Greeting

A gentle bow with palms pressed together is a polite greeting. A handshake is fine too, but a smile always works best.

🦶

Feet & Head

Don't point your feet at people or sacred objects. Avoid touching someone's head — it's considered the most sacred part of the body.

📸

Ask Before Photographing

Always ask permission before photographing people, especially monks and children. A small smile or nod usually gets a yes.

🧧

Tipping & Bargaining

Tipping is not required but appreciated. Bargain politely at markets, but never push too hard — a fair price helps the whole community.

🍽️

At the Table

Dishes are shared. Use the serving spoon, don't overfill your plate, and let elders start first. It's polite to try everything offered.
