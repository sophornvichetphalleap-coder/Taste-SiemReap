Food & Restaurants

Start with Amok (coconut curry steamed in banana leaf), Kuy Teav (rice noodle soup for breakfast), Lok Lak (stir-fried beef with pepper-lime dip), and Nom Banh Chok (Khmer noodles with fish gravy). These are the essentials every visitor should taste.

Prices & Payment

Street food: $1–$3. Local restaurants: $3–$6 per dish. Mid-range restaurants: $6–$12. Fine dining: $15–$40+. Our site lists verified price ranges for every restaurant we recommend.

Both work — Cambodia uses US dollars and Riel side by side. Small USD bills ($1, $5, $10) are most useful. You'll often get change in Riel (roughly 4,000 Riel = $1).

Upscale restaurants and hotels accept cards. Smaller places and street stalls are cash only. Mobile payment (Bakong, ABA Pay, Wing) is widely used locally, but as a tourist cash is your safest bet.

Tipping is not required but appreciated. Rounding up the bill or leaving $0.50–$2 for good service is a kind gesture. In upscale restaurants, 5–10% is a nice touch.

At markets and street stalls — yes, bargaining is expected and friendly. Smile, stay respectful, and don't push too hard. In restaurants, prices are fixed. Use our verified price ranges as a reference.

Travel & Safety

Yes — Siem Reap is generally very safe. Violent crime against tourists is rare. Use common sense: keep valuables secure, avoid isolated areas late at night, and be cautious of occasional bag-snatching by motorbike.

Most nationalities can get a visa on arrival (USD $30) or apply online via the official e-Visa portal. Bring a passport photo and ensure your passport is valid for at least 6 months.

November to March is cool and dry — the peak season and best weather. April to May is hot (up to 38°C). June to October is the green season with afternoon showers and lush landscapes.

Tuk-tuks are the classic choice ($2–$4 in town, $15–$20 for a full temple day). Grab (ride-hailing app) is reliable and metered. Bicycles and e-bikes are great for the city and nearby temples. Most of the center is walkable.

We strongly recommend it. Medical facilities in Siem Reap are limited for serious issues — most travelers are evacuated to Bangkok. Good travel insurance covers medical, trip interruption, and lost belongings.

Cover your shoulders and knees. Remove hats and shoes before entering shrines. Speak quietly, don't touch carvings or monks, and always ask before photographing people. A little respect goes a long way.
